# Nutrition Fundamentals

## The macronutrients
Food provides energy through three macronutrients, and all three matter:

- **Carbohydrates:** the body's main energy source. Whole-food sources like whole grains, fruits, vegetables, and legumes also provide fiber.
- **Protein:** used to build and repair tissue, including muscle. Sources include beans, lentils, eggs, dairy, fish, poultry, tofu, and nuts.
- **Fats:** needed for hormones, cell health, and absorbing certain vitamins. Unsaturated fats (olive oil, nuts, avocado, fish) are generally emphasized.

## Micronutrients
Vitamins and minerals are needed in small amounts but are essential. Eating a **variety of colorful fruits and vegetables** is a simple way to cover a broad range of them.

## Building a balanced plate
A commonly taught, non-restrictive way to think about a meal:

- Fill about **half the plate with vegetables and fruit**.
- About **a quarter with a protein source**.
- About **a quarter with whole grains or starchy foods**.
- Add a little healthy fat, and choose water as the default drink.

## Hydration
Water supports nearly every function in the body. A common general guideline is to drink enough that you're rarely thirsty and your urine is pale. Needs go up with heat and exercise.

## Fiber
Fiber (from vegetables, fruit, whole grains, beans, and nuts) supports digestion and helps you feel full. Most people benefit from eating more of it.

## Practical principles
- Favor **whole, minimally processed foods** most of the time.
- No single food is "magic," and no single food needs to be forbidden — overall patterns matter more than any one meal.
- Cooking at home more often gives you more control over ingredients.

*This is general educational information, not personalized dietary advice. For guidance specific to your health, consult a doctor or registered dietitian.*
