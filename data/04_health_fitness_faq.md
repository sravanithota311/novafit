# Health & Fitness FAQ

**Q: How much exercise should adults get each week?**
General guidance suggests at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week, plus muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days. Any movement is better than none.

**Q: How many hours of sleep do adults need?**
Most adults do best with about 7–9 hours per night, with consistent timing and good quality.

**Q: What are the main types of exercise?**
Cardio (endurance), strength (resistance), flexibility (stretching), and balance. A well-rounded routine includes some of each.

**Q: Do I need to give up any foods to eat healthily?**
Not usually. Overall eating patterns matter more than any single food. Favoring whole, minimally processed foods most of the time — while still enjoying treats — is a sustainable approach.

**Q: How much water should I drink?**
A simple guideline is to drink enough that you're rarely thirsty and your urine stays pale. Needs increase with heat and exercise.

**Q: Is it normal to be sore after working out?**
Mild muscle soreness a day or two after new or harder activity is common. Sharp or lasting pain is a signal to rest and, if it continues, seek advice.

**Q: How important are rest days?**
Very. Muscles get stronger during recovery, not just during exercise, so rest days are part of an effective routine.

**Q: What's a simple way to build a balanced meal?**
Fill about half your plate with vegetables and fruit, a quarter with protein, and a quarter with whole grains, plus a little healthy fat and water to drink.

**Q: What's the single best habit for health?**
There isn't one — but consistent sleep, regular movement, balanced eating, and managing stress together have the biggest impact.

*This FAQ is general educational information, not medical advice. For guidance specific to your situation, consult a qualified healthcare professional.*
