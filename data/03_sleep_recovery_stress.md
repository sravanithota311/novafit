# Sleep, Recovery, and Stress

## Why sleep matters
Sleep is when the body repairs tissue, consolidates memory, and regulates hormones and mood. It's one of the biggest levers for overall health and fitness.

## How much sleep
General guidance suggests most **adults need about 7–9 hours per night**. Teenagers typically need more. Quality matters as well as quantity — consistent, uninterrupted sleep is more restorative.

## Habits that support better sleep
- Keep a **consistent sleep and wake time**, even on weekends.
- Make the bedroom **dark, quiet, and cool**.
- Reduce **screens and bright light** in the hour before bed.
- Limit **caffeine in the afternoon and evening**.
- Get some **daylight during the day**, which helps regulate your body clock.

## Recovery after exercise
- **Rest days** let muscles rebuild and reduce injury risk.
- **Gentle movement** (walking, light stretching) on rest days can aid recovery.
- **Hydration and balanced meals** support the repair process.
- Muscle soreness a day or two after new activity is normal; persistent or sharp pain is not.

## Managing everyday stress
Chronic stress affects sleep, appetite, and health. Commonly recommended, low-cost strategies include:

- **Regular physical activity**, which is a well-supported stress reducer.
- **Breathing exercises or mindfulness**, even a few minutes a day.
- **Social connection** and time away from screens.
- **Breaking big tasks into small steps** to reduce feeling overwhelmed.

If stress, mood, or sleep problems feel persistent or overwhelming, talking to a healthcare professional is a good step.
