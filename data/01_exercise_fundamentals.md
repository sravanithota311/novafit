# Exercise Fundamentals

## The main types of exercise
A well-rounded routine usually includes four kinds of movement:

- **Cardiovascular (aerobic):** activities like brisk walking, running, cycling, or swimming that raise your heart rate and build endurance.
- **Strength (resistance):** using weights, resistance bands, or your own body weight to build muscle and bone strength.
- **Flexibility:** stretching that helps joints move through their full range of motion.
- **Balance:** movements that improve stability, especially important as people age.

## General activity guidelines for adults
Widely used public-health guidance (such as from the WHO and CDC) suggests that most adults aim for:

- At least **150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week** (for example, 30 minutes on 5 days), or 75 minutes of vigorous activity.
- **Muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days per week**, working the major muscle groups.

Any amount of movement is better than none, and it's fine to build up gradually.

## Warm-up and cool-down
- A **warm-up** (5–10 minutes of light activity) raises your heart rate gradually and prepares muscles, reducing injury risk.
- A **cool-down** with gentle movement and stretching helps your heart rate return to normal.

## Rest and recovery
Muscles get stronger during rest, not just during exercise. Most routines include **rest days**, and it's common to alternate the muscle groups you train so each has time to recover. Sleep and nutrition are a big part of recovery too.

## Tips for beginners
- Start small and increase gradually — consistency matters more than intensity at first.
- Pick activities you enjoy, so the habit sticks.
- Good form is more important than heavy weight; consider guidance from a qualified trainer when starting strength work.
- Listen to your body. Some muscle soreness is normal; sharp pain is a signal to stop.
